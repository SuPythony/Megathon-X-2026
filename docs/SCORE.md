# Green Home Score: method, mapping tables and overlap justification

_Generated by `scripts/build_score_doc.py` from `contracts/score_mapping.json` v0.4.0. Do not edit by hand. Decisions, proofs and worked examples: `docs/score/DECISIONS.md`; standards research: `docs/score/GREEN_SCORE_STANDARDS.md`; learned-weight companion: `docs/score/LEARNED_WEIGHTS.md`._

## 1. Formula

S = Σ_k w_k · s_k, with the problem statement's fixed weights w_k and sub-scores s_k in [0, 100]. Round final weighted sum to nearest integer (half up) before classification. Every sub-score is monotonic: a better state never gets a lower sub-score (enforced by sweeps in `tests/test_score.py`).

Uncertain inputs (metered intensity from the calibrated twin, missing loft depth or lighting share, a missing rating) are sampled, so the score is reported as a `Distribution` with band probabilities, e.g. "38 (90% CI 33–44), P(Poor) = 0.81". Missing data is sampled from documented priors, never set to 0.

## 2. Sub-score mappings

### 2.1 Energy efficiency (0.25)

- Input: EPC SAP score (1-100); if only band is known use band midpoint.
- Rule: sub_score = SAP score (already 1-100, monotonic).
- After a retrofit: After a retrofit there is no lodged EPC: the sub-score uses the documented post-retrofit rating estimator (contracts/sap_estimator.json), labelled 'estimated rating, not an EPC'.
- Why: Problem statement: energy efficiency must reflect the recorded energy rating (or a documented rating-based measure).

### 2.2 Energy intensity (0.20)

- Input: Annualised metered energy per m2, as defined in the problem statement: (grid electricity import + gas, kWh over the available months, annualised) / total floor area. NOT weather-normalised.
- Annualisation: Sum of available months scaled to 12 months using each missing month's typical share of annual use (degree-day shares for gas, flat shares for electricity); months with no data are never treated as zero; < 6 months -> flagged low confidence; 0 months -> intensity unavailable, sub-score taken from the twin-modelled intensity with a warning and a wider interval.
- Sensitivity: A weather-normalised intensity (typical-year degree days) is reported alongside as a sensitivity, not used in the score.
- Direction: lower is better. Why: Metered and per m2, so large homes are not penalised for size alone; distinct from the rating-based energy-efficiency component.

### 2.7 Smart energy management (0.05)

- sub_score = (1 - lighting_share) x controls_level + lighting_share x low_energy_lighting_pct (0-100). Missing lighting % is sampled from the EPC prior, never 0.
- controls_level = highest level whose requirements are all present.

| Controls present | Controls level |
| --- | --- |
| none | 0 |
| programmer | 20 |
| programmer, room_thermostat | 40 |
| programmer, room_thermostat, trvs | 60 |
| smart_thermostat | 75 |
| smart_thermostat, trvs | 90 |
| smart_thermostat, zoned_control, smart_meter | 100 |

Lighting: The brief fixes seven components, so lighting joins this one as 'controls and efficient electricity use'. lighting_share is learned (decision D-26): simplex NNLS on RdSAP 10 certificates, lighting share of this component 0.463 (SAP target) and 0.472 (primary energy); adding lighting raises held-out R2 on all four modelled targets. Overlap with energy efficiency (SAP already counts lighting) is accepted on the same basis as insulation: the rating is an outcome, components are levers.

## 3. Overlaps and why they are kept

| Overlap | Components | Kept because | How big |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Solar PV | renewable energy + energy intensity | Solar PV affects two components: renewable_energy credits installed generation capacity, and energy_intensity falls because PV self-consumption reduces metered grid import. We keep both because the problem statement defines intensity on metered energy and renewables as a separate lever. Justification: intensity measures what the home actually buys; renewables rewards on-site generation the meter does not see (exports and capacity). Disclosure: the evaluation reports the score with and without PV self-consumption added back to intensity, so the size of the overlap is visible. | Example below: 0.86 points via intensity vs 8.5 via renewables |
| Fabric and heating vs rating | insulation, heating, glazing + energy efficiency | Insulation is an actionable lever; overlap with the EPC rating is accepted and documented: the rating is an overall modelled outcome, components are the levers customers can change. | Bounded by the weights: the five lever components share 0.55 |
| Lighting vs rating | smart energy management + energy efficiency | SAP already counts lighting; the component rewards the lever. Learned share, decision D-26 | ≤ 0.05 × 0.47 × 100 = 2.4 points |

### PV overlap, sized

Every `ScoreResult` carries `pv_overlap_points` in the energy-intensity component's inputs: the intensity sub-score with self-consumed PV added back (default self-consumption share 40%, LLD 4.1.1), times its weight. Worked example (4 kWp, 850 kWh/kWp/yr, 95 m², metered 170 kWh/m²): generation 3,400 kWh/yr, self-consumed 14.3 kWh/m²; intensity sub-score changes by 4.3 → **0.86 score points** of overlap, against 8.5 points from the renewables component. The score is shown with and without PV added back.

## 4. Post-retrofit (projected) score

The projected score re-scores the home after a package: the recorded features are edited by `greentwin/retrofit/property_edits.py`, intensity comes from the twin's package run (anchored to the meters), and energy efficiency uses the `sap_estimator.json` `RatingEstimate`, labelled "estimated rating, not an EPC". A package that only improves inputs never lowers the score (test `test_property_after_and_projected_score_not_lower`).

## 5. Tests that guard this document

- `tests/test_score.py`: monotonicity sweeps on every sub-score, every heating system mapped, weights sum to 1, half-up rounding, band of the rounded score, missing data widens never zeroes, projected ≥ current.
- `tests/test_retrofit_engine.py`: projected score after a package is not lower.
